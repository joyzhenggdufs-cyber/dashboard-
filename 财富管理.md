# Personal Wealth Management Framework (Finance OS v1)

## Purpose and context

This document is the operating context for an AI agent supporting the user's personal wealth management. It records the current framework, priorities, and decision rules. The aim is not to optimize every transaction; it is to continually strengthen the overall asset structure, cash flow, and use of attention over time.

**Core philosophy:** Wealth management is a system for improving asset allocation, liquidity, and attention—not a contest to predict every market move correctly.

## Financial objectives

1. **Asset safety / long-term foundation**
   - Maintain long-term security assets that should generally not be used for short-term spending or market deployment.
2. **Wealth growth**
   - Grow the securities account from the current 400,000+ RMB range toward a **1,000,000 RMB** target within the year.
   - Treat this as a distinct investment objective, not as a substitute for the full household balance sheet.
3. **Liquidity and cash-flow stability**
   - Preserve sufficient deployable cash for known needs before pursuing investment return.
   - Current key requirement: maintain **200,000 RMB of cash available for home construction** in May.

## Three-statement operating system

Update these statements monthly rather than attempting detailed daily bookkeeping.

### 1. Balance sheet — “What do I have?”

- Assets by category and account
- Liabilities, including credit-card balances
- Net worth and changes from the prior month

### 2. Income and expense statement — “What did I create and spend this month?”

- Monthly income
- Major spending items
- Net monthly surplus / deficit

### 3. Cash-flow statement — “Is my cash safe?”

- Immediately available cash, including bank balances, WeChat, Alipay, and available brokerage cash
- Near-term commitments and known large payments
- Cash coverage for the coming month(s)

## Asset classification

### Cash layer

Includes bank accounts, WeChat, Alipay, and available funds in brokerage accounts.

**Role:** everyday spending, emergency resilience, and opportunity capital. Liquidity has priority over return when upcoming cash needs exist.

### Growth layer

Includes equities, funds, and gold.

**Role:** long-term net-worth growth, managed through allocation and risk control rather than short-term price prediction.

### Foundation layer

## Investment principles

- **Position management over cost-basis management.** Ask what proportion of total assets an investment represents, rather than fixating on its purchase price or unrealized gain/loss.
- **ETF-oriented long-term allocation.** Prefer diversified ETFs as the long-term default because they reduce single-company risk, emotional trading, and monitoring burden.
- **Avoid duplicated risk.** Do not create multiple holdings driven by the same underlying thesis. For example, internet, AI, or semiconductor exposure in individual stocks should be considered alongside thematic funds and ETFs before adding more.
- **Attention is an asset.** A holding that requires daily monitoring has an attention cost. Its expected benefit must justify that cost.
- **System over prediction.** Rely on allocation limits, rebalancing, and risk rules rather than trying to forecast short-term price direction.
- **Liquidity before return.** Known cash requirements must be fully covered before increasing investment risk.

## Monthly update protocol

At month-end, provide only the following information:

- Account balances
- Income received during the month
- Major expenditures
- Investment actions taken (buys, sells, rebalancing)
- Credit-card liabilities
- Known cash requirements for the next month

The reviewing agent should then:

1. Update the three financial statements.
2. Check whether the cash layer covers known obligations.
3. Recalculate allocation by asset class and major risk exposure.
4. Flag duplicated exposures, concentration risk, or conflicts with near-term liquidity needs.
5. Recommend only actions that directly support the stated objectives and rules.

## Investment decision checklist

Before buying, selling, or materially increasing any asset, answer:

1. **What role does it serve?** Growth, defense, liquidity, or future cash flow?
2. **What percentage of total assets will it represent after the action?**
3. **Does it duplicate an existing risk exposure or investment thesis?**
4. **What attention will it require?** Is that ongoing attention justified?
5. **Would it still be reasonable to hold for five years?** If not, treat it as a deliberate trade rather than a strategic allocation.

## Current asset-management philosophy

- Protect the long-term foundation; do not casually draw down provident funds, pension assets, or savings insurance.
- Keep the securities-growth objective separate from essential household liquidity.
- Use diversified, lower-maintenance holdings as the default and reserve individual-stock or thematic risk for intentionally sized positions.
- View cash as a strategic asset when home construction or other predictable large expenses are approaching.
- Measure progress by resilience, allocation quality, liquidity coverage, and sustainable attention demands—not just short-term returns.
